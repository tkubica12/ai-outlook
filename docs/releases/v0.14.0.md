# Tomlook v0.14.0

This release adds scheduling with other people: invitations with live
availability, finding a time for a group, proposing it in Teams or by e-mail
and holding the candidate times with BLOCKERs. These are changes since
[v0.13.0](https://github.com/tkubica12/ai-outlook/releases/tag/v0.13.0).
v0.8.0 and later install it automatically after verification when you choose
**Restart now** in Settings → Updates or exit Tomlook.

## Invite attendees and see their availability

The new-event editor has an **Attendees** (**Účastníci**) field. Suggestions
come from people in your calendar (by how often and how recently you met) and,
when WorkIQ is connected, from a read-only directory search. Each attendee can
be required or optional; you, your other mailboxes, rooms and duplicates are
never added.

Below the time sliders, a scheduling grid shows you and every attendee from
08:00 to 18:00 as busy, tentative, out of office, working elsewhere, free or
unknown, using Microsoft 365 free/busy. Hover shows a visible subject; click
moves the start time. A summary line such as "1 of 3 available · Jamie
tentative · Dana unknown" and **Suggest times** help pick a slot.

**Create and send invitations** (**Vytvořit a odeslat pozvánky**) is the only
action that sends Outlook invitations, after the dialog lists exactly who will
receive them. The created meeting is read back and must contain exactly the
intended attendees. An uncertain outcome is never retried automatically; check
Outlook first, because invitations may already have been sent.

## Find a time

**Find a time** (**Najít termín**) opens from Commands, from the event editor
or from a meeting's menu ("Find another time with these people"). Choose a
topic, attendees, duration, date range and daily window. Tomlook reads
free/busy and ranks times where all required people are free, then by
unknown, tentative and optional conflicts, avoiding lunch and spreading
candidates across days. Unknown availability is never treated as free.

Select up to five times, then:

- **Create meeting** for one time, through the normal invitation approval;
- **Hold with BLOCKERs** creates one busy `BLOCKER: <topic>` event per time
  in your own calendar, without attendees, after listing them for approval;
- **Propose in a Teams group chat** creates a chat with exactly the selected
  people (1:1 for one person) and sends one editable message listing the
  times, each with its UTC offset (for example "10:00–10:30 (UTC+01:00)").
  The approval shows members, topic and the full message; anyone who
  cannot be resolved in your directory stops the send;
- **Propose by e-mail** prepares local text, with the same times and UTC
  offsets, for **Copy** or **Open in Outlook**; Tomlook never sends e-mail
  itself.

## Close the loop

BLOCKERs created by Tomlook show **Proposed time**. **Confirm this time**
opens the invitation editor for that slot and afterwards offers to remove the
proposal's BLOCKERs; **Release all BLOCKERs** removes them. Only events that
match the stored event ID, transaction ID and `BLOCKER:` title are ever
deleted. BLOCKERs count as busy time but not as meetings in My day or the
weekly review, and they are never prepared by AI.

All of these writes are single-use approvals that are read back after
writing; none of them are available to the AI assistant.

## Download and upgrade

v0.8.0 and later update automatically. Otherwise download
`Tomlook-v0.14.0-win-x64.zip`, fully exit Tomlook (including its tray
instance) and replace `Tomlook.exe`. Keep the existing `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Tomlook`
profile.

## Verification scope

Offline tests cover attendee handling, free/busy requests in batches of 20,
parsing, candidate ranking including daylight-saving days, invitation and
BLOCKER read-back, Teams request shapes, approvals, deletion matching and
persisted outcomes. Live read-only probes confirmed free/busy and suggested
times for the signed-in mailbox and that WorkIQ supports creating Teams chats
and messages. No invitation, BLOCKER, chat or message was created during
development, so live writes have not yet been observed.
